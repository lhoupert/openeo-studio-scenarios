# Presenter notes: NW Mediterranean deep winters

A short script for presenting this scenario, about 3-4 minutes, with three of the four projects. The full
explanation, with methods and references, is in the [scenario README](README.md) and each project's README.

Open each project in a **fresh tab** (close the previous Studio tab first: Studio tabs share their map
services) and show **one layer at a time** (untick the previous one: ticked layers blend).

**Hook** (2013 sequence as it opens, the Gulf of Lion between Spain, France and Corsica): "Every few winters,
the Mistral and the Tramontane chill this part of the Mediterranean so much that the surface water sinks and
the sea mixes from the surface to the seafloor, more than 2 km down. It's one of the few places on Earth where
this happens. Can we see it from space?"

## 1. One winter, week by week

Project: <https://studio.mpc.ds.io/editor/exp-nw-med-deep-convection-2013-modis-8-day-sequence>

1. **25 Jan-1 Feb** (opening layer)
   - **Show:** the purple-blue patch in the middle of the gulf.
   - **Say:** "This is the colour of the sea, turned into a chlorophyll index. The mixing drags the algae of
     the surface down into the dark, so the mixed area shows up as a patch with almost no chlorophyll."
2. **26 Feb-5 Mar** (layer 5)
   - **Show:** the patch at its widest, stretched south-west to north-east.
   - **Say:** "End of February: the mixing reaches the seafloor, and the patch is the size of a small country,
     tens of thousands of square kilometres."
3. **6-13 Mar, then 14-21 Mar** (layers 6 and 7)
   - **Show:** a ring of green around a shrinking patch; a week later, the patch is back.
   - **Say:** "The wind drops and the algae start growing at the edges. Then a new cold spell mixes the sea
     again and the patch comes back."
4. **7-14 Apr** (layer 10)
   - **Show:** orange and red over the whole basin.
   - **Say:** "In April the algae bloom on the nutrients the mixing brought up from the deep: the largest
     bloom of the Mediterranean."

## 2. Is every winter like this?

Project: <https://studio.mpc.ds.io/editor/exp-nw-med-deep-winters-2005-2018-compared-modis-patch>
(opens on 2013)

- **Show:** 2013, then 2007 (no patch), then 2005 (the widest), then 2018 (smaller, part under cloud).
- **Say:** "Same satellite, same colours, one image per winter. The patch is huge in some winters and absent
  in others: in 2007 gliders saw the mixing stop at about 400 m."

## 3. What happened since?

Project: <https://studio.mpc.ds.io/editor/exp-nw-med-recent-winters-2019-2026-vs-2013-and-2018-modis-patch>
(opens on 2013)

- **Show:** 2013, then 2019 (a small patch), then 2024 (nothing).
- **Say:** "Since 2014 the mixing has mostly stayed above about 500 m, and the patch has shrunk or vanished.
  Weaker mixing means less deep water formed and fewer nutrients brought up."

**Take-home:** a free, cloud-optimised archive of 8-day satellite images is enough to follow a deep-ocean
process from space, week by week and winter by winter, and to see it fading in recent years. The images do
not give the depth: that comes from moorings, gliders and floats.

## Practical tips

- **Zoom:** stay around the opening view. Studio shows nothing below about map zoom 4.5.
- **Load times:** a few seconds per layer. The weeks project (low-chl weeks) is slower the first time (up to
  about 25 s per tile): open it before presenting if you use it.
- **Colours:** red along the coast is murky river water as much as algae; point at the open sea.
- **Old tabs:** if a project looks out of date, open it in a new tab, and don't Save from the old one (it
  would put the old version back).
