# Presenter notes: North Water polynya, 2026

A short script for presenting this scenario, about 2-3 minutes. The full explanation of each layer, with
methods and references, is in this scenario's [README](README.md).

Project: <https://studio.mpc.ds.io/editor/exp-north-water-polynya-2026-sentinel-1-radar>
(opens on layer 3, the 9 May picture). Open it in a **fresh tab** and show **one layer at a time** (untick the
previous one: ticked layers blend).

**Hook** (opening layer, 9 May true colour):
- **Orient:** Ellesmere Island on the left, Greenland on the right. Everything is white ice, except a black
  tongue down the middle, ending in a sharp curved edge at the top.
- **Say:** "This is the North Water, the largest polynya in the Arctic: open water or thin ice in the
  middle of the winter sea ice. That curved edge is an ice arch. Ice coming from the north jams there like a
  cork in a bottle. South of it, wind and currents keep pushing the ice away."

Then go back in time.

1. **22 Mar-15 Apr, radar change**
   - **Say:** "How do we know the ice north of the edge is stuck? Radar sees through cloud, day or night.
     Here we stack three radar images from the same orbit, taken 12 days apart, as red, green and blue."
   - **Show:** "Grey means identical on all three dates: the ice didn't move. Colour means the surface
     changed, which is the polynya. The line between grey and colour is the arch."
2. **9 Apr, surface temperature**
   - **Say:** "Landsat measures surface temperature."
   - **Show:** a warm tongue that starts at the arch. Yellow, about -2 C, is open water; orange to purple is
     thin new ice; blue is thick ice and land below about -23 C.
   - **Say:** "Open water in the middle of an Arctic winter loses heat to the air, far more than the ice
     around it."
3. **9 May, true colour:** back to the opening picture: the same arch at the same place, now in true
   colour.
4. **25 Jun, true colour**
   - **Show:** the edge is gone, open water reaches further north, and loose melting floes lie in Kane Basin
     and the former polynya.
   - **Say:** "When it forms, the arch usually breaks up in June or July."

**Take-home:** three kinds of satellite image, each answering a different question: is the ice moving
(radar)? Is the surface warm (thermal)? What does it look like (optical)? The 2026 timing comes from
satellite images alone.

## Practical tips

- **Zoom:** don't zoom out too far. Studio shows nothing below about map zoom 4.5.
- **Load times:** the first view of the radar-change layer takes about 10 seconds. Mention it, or open each
  layer once before presenting.
- **Old tabs:** if the project looks out of date, open it in a new tab, and don't Save from the old one (it
  would put the old version back).
